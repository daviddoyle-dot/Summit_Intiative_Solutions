---
title: "Take Care of Your Stuff"
date: "2026-12-22"
summary: "Stewardship means using what you have with an eye on what the next team inherits."
---

Effectively led, well-prepared people can accomplish amazing things when coordinated through a known process. Talented people—and how leaders harness that talent—make a difference in any organization’s success.

But don’t forget: people use resources to get things done.

In many organizations where I served, equipment, material, and supplies directly enabled mission accomplishment. Conservation, maintenance, and care of those resources were often essential for survival.

However, I often had to remind my people that “taking care of our stuff” had a temporal component as well.

As an aside, my Mom always seemed annoyed that I used the term “stuff.” She would have preferred something more erudite—and she was probably right.

What I was really trying to communicate was stewardship.

We weren’t using what we had like a rental—we were using it with an understanding of what those who followed us would inherit.

Would the office be up to date and well cared for?
Would our vehicles be ready for the next decade of operations?
Would our budgets reflect the discipline required to support future missions?
Had we developed a leader training plan that prepared this generation’s junior leaders to become the next generation’s senior leaders?

Stewardship matters. It is a sign of a mature, thriving organization.

What are you leaving for those who will follow in your footsteps?

Design an organization that understands stewardship, and you will design an organization that not only gets things done—but gets them done in the right direction.
