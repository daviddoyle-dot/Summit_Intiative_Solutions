title: "Trust Is Not a Personality"
date: "2026-12-08"

---

“Trust matters.”

Most leaders agree with that statement.

Far fewer organizations actually try to evaluate it.

Trust is often treated as something intangible—something that simply appears when the right personalities align at the right moment.

But if trust only exists because the right personalities happen to be in the right positions at the right time, then trust is not an organizational strength—it is an organizational dependency.

Organizations that truly care about trust establish ways to evaluate it.

Not perfectly.
Not mechanically.
But intentionally.

In my previous organization, the 4th Infantry Division, I used Operations Research and Systems Analysts (ORSAs) to help gather synthesized data derived from:

- Safety and serious incident trends
- Surveys and sensing sessions
- Re-enlistment and retention trends
- Maintenance and training readiness
- Out-briefs from departing Soldiers

The goal was never to replace leadership judgment with spreadsheets.

The goal was to inform leadership intuition about levels of trust with observable data rather than relying solely on subjective opinion.

Trust reveals itself through organizational behavior. People in high-trust organizations communicate problems faster, admit mistakes earlier, share information more openly, collaborate more effectively, and adapt more quickly under pressure.

I learned this clearly in Army Aviation.

![Flying a UH-60M Black Hawk](/uploads/blog/2026/uh60m-cockpit-1200x1600.jpg)

Flying under the supervision of highly experienced instructor pilots and crew chiefs reinforced how operational trust is built and sustained.

A helicopter crew includes a pilot in command, a pilot, and crew chiefs—all with different responsibilities but all essential to mission success.

The pilot in command is responsible for everything but cannot see everything.
Cannot do everything.
Cannot monitor everything.

Trust is operationally essential.

But that trust is not personality-based.

It is reinforced through:

- Training
- Certification
- Evaluation
- Standards
- Retraining
- Accountability

Organizations that truly value trust do more than talk about it.

They build systems that reinforce it, evaluate it, and sustain it over time.
